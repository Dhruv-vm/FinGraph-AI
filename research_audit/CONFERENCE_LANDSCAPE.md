## 1. Demystifying Conference Ranking and Indexing Systems

When evaluating international publication venues, researchers must distinguish between ranking, reputation, publisher, and indexing database:

### 1. CORE Ranking (Computing Research and Education Association of Australasia)
- **CORE A* (Top ~4%):** Flagship global conferences (e.g., ACL, KDD, SIGIR, NeurIPS, ICML). Extremely competitive (15–22% acceptance rate). Submissions require theoretical breakthroughs or massive-scale evaluations across dozens of standard benchmarks.
- **CORE A (Top ~15%):** Highly prestigious, well-cited international venues (e.g., CIKM, EMNLP, IEEE BigData, COLING). Excellent visibility, high rigor, strong acceptance standards (20–28%).
- **CORE B (Next ~25%):** Established international conferences with reputable peer review (e.g., ICTAI, IEEE ICASSP, ECIR). Highly respected, realistic for rigorous applied AI/NLP research (28–35%).
- **CORE C:** Regional or emerging conferences with lower citation impact.

### 2. Indexing Databases vs Publishers
- **Publishers:** IEEE, ACM, Springer, Elsevier produce the physical and digital proceedings volumes.
- **Digital Libraries:** **IEEE Xplore** and **ACM Digital Library** host the official full-text papers, assign registered DOIs, and ensure permanent archival preservation.
- **Indexing Databases:** **Scopus (Elsevier)** and **Web of Science (Clarivate CPCI)** index papers post-publication, tracking citations and h-index metrics. Having proceedings indexed in Scopus and IEEE Xplore is the gold standard for university and graduate admission committees worldwide.
- **Key Caveat:** "IEEE Sponsored" vs "IEEE Indexed": An IEEE technically co-sponsored conference is reviewed under IEEE quality guidelines and published in IEEE Xplore, which is subsequently indexed in Scopus. Avoid predatory unranked conferences claiming "instant publication".

## 2. Realistic Conference Strategy for FinGraph AI

FinGraph AI has strong genuine engineering, high factual rigor (FEIR = 0.0%, 0% hallucinations, 10,054 KG nodes, 8,181 chunks), and an innovative Entity-Balanced RRF mechanism. However, as an undergraduate-led research project with a specialized 64-question financial benchmark and an auxiliary walk-forward experiment, targeting CORE A* venues (e.g. ACL or KDD main conference) carries high rejection risk due to reviewer demands for 100k+ question benchmarks across 5 languages.

**Optimal Target Profile:**
- **Tier 1 (Reach):** CORE A venues (CIKM, IEEE BigData).
- **Tier 2 (Strong Realistic Match):** Premier Applied Financial AI Venues (ACM ICAIF) or CORE B venues (IEEE ICTAI, FinNLP Workshop at EMNLP/COLING).
- **Tier 3 (Fast, High-Impact Journal Backup):** IEEE Access (Q1, Impact Factor ~3.4, Scopus/WoS indexed, 4-6 week review turnaround).
